IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA
CIVIL APPELLATE JURISDICTION

Civil Appeal No. 6237 of 1990

Lucknow Development Authority ... Appellant
Versus
M.K. Gupta ... Respondent

JUDGMENT

R.M. SAHAI, J.

1. The question of law that arises for consideration in these appeals is whether the statutory authorities constituted under state housing and development acts are amenable to the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 for any deficiency in service.

2. The respondent had applied for allotment of a flat and deposited the required installments. Despite full payment, possession was unlawfully withheld for several years without any valid explanation. The District Forum awarded compensation, which was upheld by the State Commission and National Commission.

3. We have carefully considered the statutory framework. The definition of 'consumer' and 'service' under the Consumer Protection Act is wide enough to encompass activities undertaken by housing authorities in constructing and delivering flats. A statutory authority discharging sovereign functions may be immune, but when performing commercial activities such as housing development, it renders a service for consideration.

4. When a citizen is made to wait indefinitely for possession after parting with his hard-earned savings, it amounts to grave deficiency in service and harassment. The Commission has full jurisdiction to award compensation in such cases.

5. The appeals are accordingly dismissed with costs.
